## INVOICE-010

An invoice, estimate, sales receipt (SALES-001) or recurring template can carry three further kinds of line: - A discount, as a percentage or an amount, on the subtotal or on chosen lines. It applies before tax by default, or after tax where the business's state treats it so, and posts to a discounts account the accountant chose. - A shipping or delivery charge with its own taxability. - A deposit paid at issue, with method, date and deposit-to account. It reduces the balance due and appears on the PDF. The exact semantics: a subtotal discount applies to the item lines, never to delivery charges, while a discount on chosen lines may name any of the invoice's lines, a delivery charge included. Several discounts apply in the order given, each on what the earlier ones left. A percentage is more than 0% and at most 100%, with up to three decimals, and the discount rounds half up to the cent. A before-tax discount is spread over its lines in proportion to their remaining amounts by largest remainder (ties to the lower line), and each line's tax is its rate on its amount less its share, rounded half up. An after-tax discount leaves every line's tax on the undiscounted price; its percentage is of its lines' pre-tax amounts. The person drafting chooses after tax; Pantry never infers a state's treatment, and approval covers the choice. At issue the discounts post as one debit to the discounts account. Someone with the post grant chooses that account (an active income or expense account), and it is fixed on a draft when the draft is saved: a draft saved before an account was chosen cannot be approved. Credits carry ordinary item lines only. A deposit is no more than the total and is dated on or before the issue date. At issue it becomes a payment by its method into its deposit-to account, applied to the invoice on the issue date. A recurring template carries its discounts, delivery charges and deposit to each invoice it creates, and that deposit is dated the invoice's issue date. Estimates (INVOICE-006) and sales receipts (SALES-001) inherit these lines before they ship. An early-payment term, for example 2% 10, net 30, is shown on the hosted page and applied automatically when the invoice is paid within the window. It follows ct's ruling of 20 October 2026: - **Treatment per tax code.** Each code's early-payment treatment is undecided until someone with the post grant records retain (the discount reduces only the price; taxable sales and tax stay as invoiced) or reduce (a taken discount also reduces taxable sales and tax), under SALESTAX-001. Neither is universal, and Pantry never guesses a jurisdiction or a treatment. - **The term and its offer.** Only invoices carry a term, including recurring ones and, once INVOICE-006 ships, converted ones; credits, write-offs, sales and refund receipts, imported and opening invoices never do. The percentage is more than 0% and less than 100% with up to three decimals; the window is 1 to the number of days until the due date, in whole days, and ends on the issue date plus that many calendar days, inclusive, as business days in the sending time zone. The offer is computed in integer cents when the draft is saved: each line's net nᵢ is its price after these discounts (delivery included, tax and deposits excluded); B = Σnᵢ; D = B × p rounded half up and spread over the lines by largest remainder, ties to the lower line; on reduce lines rᵢ = tᵢ − tax(nᵢ − dᵢ), never rounded on its own, otherwise 0; R = Σrᵢ; the amount to settle is S = total − D − R, and the amount to pay is S less the deposit received at issue, which must leave something to pay. The offer keeps each line's code, state, rate, treatment, treatment revision and source. PAN-04 approval covers the offer and is refused while a taxable line's code is undecided, when a treatment was recorded again after the draft was saved, or when no early-payment discounts account was chosen (it defaults to the discounts account). The issued PDF and hosted page say "Early-payment discount: p% if paid by ‹date›. Pay ‹amount› by then." with "Sales tax stays ‹T›." or "Sales tax on the discounted price: ‹T − R›.", per code when codes are mixed; the issued document is never recomputed. - **Taking it.** Settlements that count are standing payment allocations dated on or before the window end, the deposit received at issue, and a Stripe Checkout session created in the window for the early-pay amount that completes (a card succeeds, or an ACH payment later succeeds), judged by the session rather than the payout date; credits never count. In the same atomic write that first brings them to S, Pantry posts a system-marked credit linked to the invoice: CR- series, issued at once, relying on the invoice's approval, never markable by a client. It has one line per invoice line with dᵢ to the early-payment discounts account, the original tax code and rᵢ, referring to the original line and carrying no item, and posts Cr receivables D + R, Dr discounts Σdᵢ and Dr each tax account its rᵢ, with its own PDF titled "Early-payment discount on INV-…". It is allocated before the payment, whose allocation is capped at what remains, so any excess stays unapplied. It is dated on the date of that write and records the date taken and the window end. It is all or nothing: partial payments count only inside the window, nothing is automatic after it (the shortfall stays open and reminders continue), and the database allows one standing discount credit per invoice, so replays post nothing. Any other credit applied first withdraws the offer, and the hosted page names that credit. - **Reversal.** Reversing a counted payment (journal.reverse, a PAY-004 undo, and PAY-002 returns and refunds once they exist) that takes settlements below S reverses the discount credit in the same write: a linked reversal dated that day, after which its allocation no longer counts; no discount is granted again unless new counted settlements arrive in the window. journal.reverse refuses the credit itself; someone with the post grant may reverse it with a reason through its own action. - **Reports and locks.** The issued invoice, its tax, journal entry and PDF never change. In the sales-tax report retain lines leave taxable sales, tax and the exempt figure as invoiced and appear under "Early-payment discounts, tax kept"; reduce lines lower taxable sales by dᵢ and tax by rᵢ in the period of the discount's date on both bases, under "Early-payment discounts, tax reduced" and "Tax reduced"; a discount on an exempt line is shown for information; a reversal gives the reduction back in its own period; an item whose invoice predates the report's range is flagged as a sale in an earlier period with its issue date and state; the ledger tie holds. The nexus watch excludes discount credits, and sales by item shows them in the no-item row. Month locks refuse discount credits and their reversals as they refuse payments; filed periods follow SALESTAX-002. - **Authority and audit.** No new authority is created: a discount relies on the invoice's approval and the treatment revisions, its actor is whoever made the payment write, and agent creation or reversal carries commitment, adding money when funds move, and the company’s policy decides it. A late discount is never applied automatically; an optional honor through PAN-04 credit approval (INVOICE-008) uses the original approved offer, is dated after its window in an open period and is marked "honored after window". AUDIT-001 records treatment and discounts-account changes before and after, the offer in the draft and at approval, and each discount taken (payments and dates, window, B, D, R, lines, treatment revisions and the approval relied on), withdrawn or reversed. Pantry gives no tax advice: the table is a sourced starting point for the accountant.

## BILLPAY-002

A first payment to a vendor over the owner's threshold, a vendor whose bank details changed at the provider in the last 30 days, and an emailed bill whose sender domain imitates a known vendor's domain are each held. Release needs a call-back to a number already on file, recorded as a task, and a second approver. The look-alike rule is exact. A vendor's known domains are the domains on its vendor record and the sender domains of mail already accepted from it. Domains are compared by their registrable part under the Public Suffix List: the label before the public suffix, and the suffix itself. A sender domain is a look-alike when it is not itself a known domain of any vendor, and one of these holds against some vendor's known domain: 1. **Near spelling:** the same suffix, and a label within Damerau-Levenshtein distance 1 for labels of up to 6 characters, or 2 for longer labels (`acmeplumbimg.com` for `acmeplumbing.com`). 2. **Homoglyph:** the label's Unicode confusable skeleton (UTS #39), after decoding punycode, equals the known label's skeleton, including digit-for-letter swaps such as `1` for `l` and `0` for `o` (`acmep1umbing.com`, or a Cyrillic `а`). 3. **Other suffix:** the identical label on a different public suffix (`acmeplumbing.co`, `acmeplumbing.net`). 4. **Added word:** the known label joined by a hyphen to one more word (`acmeplumbing-billing.com`, `billing-acmeplumbing.com`). 5. **Name borrowed:** the message's display name equals a known vendor's name while the domain is none of its known domains. Mail from a shared mailbox domain (gmail.com, outlook.com, yahoo.com, icloud.com and the other entries of a versioned list) is compared by full address instead of domain, under the same rules applied to the part before the `@`. Each flag names the rule and the vendor domain it resembles.

## SALESTAX-004

Pantry totals sales and transactions by destination state over each state's measuring period. It compares them with that state's economic-nexus threshold, kept as a versioned table with its sources (for example $100,000 of sales in many states). It alerts the owner and accountant at 80% and at 100%. Whether to register is the accountant's decision, recorded in Pantry. Sales are invoice line amounts before sales tax, including exempt lines, less credits, by issue date; transactions are issued invoices (a credit is not a transaction). A sale's destination is its ship-to state, then (for a credit) its invoice's, then the state of the line's tax code; a sale with none is listed as unassigned, never guessed. Marketplace and state-specific exclusions are not deducted, so the watch errs early. Each state is measured to the business day in the installation's sending time zone over its rule's completed period and the period in progress: the previous calendar year and the current year to date; the 12 months ending today; New York's four completed sales tax quarters (March–May, June–August, September–November, December–February) and the four ending with the current one; Connecticut's 12 months ending 30 September and the twelve months in progress. A state's share is the larger across its periods; an either-test state counts the larger of its sales and transaction shares, a both-test state (Connecticut, New York) the smaller. States without a statewide sales tax (AK, DE, MT, NH, OR) are not watched. Each level alerts once by email to the owner and every active accountant and in Books › Sales tax, showing the mail outcome that actually happened; an alert resolves when the share falls back below its level, and a later crossing alerts again. Only an invited accountant records a decision (register, not required, keep watching) with a reason; each keeps the period, figures and threshold-table version it was made on, and the newest shows on the state's row. Pantry never registers or files for the business. Write-off credits (ACCT-003) are bad debts, not returns, and never reduce a state's gross sales.

## CLOSE-004

Checks run daily, not only at close. Each flag cites its lines, shows the rule and setting that raised it, and can be dismissed with a reason. Each rule's default can be changed by the owner. The checks are: - **Unreconciled account:** no reconciliation more than 45 days after its last statement date. - **Stale review queue:** a line waiting in the review queue for more than 7 days. - **Wrong-side balance:** a credit balance in receivables, or a debit balance in a card or line-of-credit account. - **Likely duplicate payment:** two bills or payments to the same vendor for the same amount within 7 days, or with the same vendor invoice number, unless they come from one recurring template. - **Amount unusual for a vendor:** judged only for a vendor with at least 4 posted bills or expenses in the previous 12 months. An amount is flagged when it is more than 3 times, or less than a third of, the median of those amounts and also differs from that median by at least $100. A vendor with fewer postings is never flagged by this check. - **Suspense:** a balance older than 7 days in an account marked suspense or "ask my accountant". - **Clearing difference:** a processor clearing account that has differed from the provider's balance in transit (PROC-003) for more than 3 days. - **Payroll mismatch:** a PAYROLL-001 or PAYROLL-002 difference. **Exact semantics (what the code does).** One run a day (the Cron trigger, or the first request after two minutes without one) judges every rule with today's date on the business's calendar (the time zone in the invoice sending settings); days are whole calendar days, "more than N days" means strictly more than N days after the date it counts from, and a run changes nothing in the books. A flag cites the records that raised it, the rule in words and the setting values it used, is first flagged on the day a run finds it, and clears itself when the condition has gone. Dismissing a flag needs a reason and the bookkeeper's record grant; it holds while the cited lines (their records, amounts and dates, or the balance it cites) are unchanged, and the flag opens again the day they change. The owner alone changes a default, and every change is audited. Defaults: 45 days (unreconciled), 7 (review queue), 7 (duplicate window), 4 postings, 3 times and $100.00 (unusual amount), 7 (suspense), 3 (clearing difference), and a 60-day look-back for the duplicate and unusual-amount checks, which judge only bills and payments dated inside it; every other check judges the books as they stand. Unreconciled account: a statement whose end date is more than the set days before today and which has no completed reconciliation (one flag per bank account). Stale review queue: a feed or statement line with no active match that the bank has not removed, counted from when it arrived (a statement's import time for file lines), one flag per bank account. Wrong-side balance: posted entries dated up to today, in an active receivable account (credit) or an active liability bank or card account (debit). Likely duplicate: two posted bills (opening balances excluded) or two payments made, not reversed, to one vendor for equal amounts at most the set days apart, or two posted bills of one vendor whose invoice numbers are equal ignoring case, spaces and punctuation; bills one recurring template made, and payments of bills one template made, are never a pair. Unusual amount: each posted bill and each posted expense (a posted entry not made by a bill, with an expense-account line naming the vendor) is judged against the same vendor's other bills and expenses dated in the 12 months before its own date (earlier dates only; an even count's median is the mean of the two middle amounts); a vendor with fewer than 4 such postings is never judged. Suspense: an account the accountant marked as a suspense or "ask my accountant" account (a setting on the account) whose end-of-day balance has stayed non-zero for more than the set days. Clearing difference: the first day a run saw the Stripe clearing account differ from Stripe's balance is kept; the flag opens once that difference has stood more than the set days and is forgotten when the difference is gone. Payroll mismatch: every payroll read from the connected provider that the books held back instead of posting (PAYROLL-001); PAYROLL-002's reconciliation differences join this check when they exist. A flag does not stop a period locking; the close checklist (PAN-13) keeps its own checks. Every future clause that adds a posting kind, a payment route, a review queue or a processor clearing account inherits the obligation to be covered by these checks before it ships, and its own acceptance names the check it feeds (BILLPAY-001, PAY-005, SALES-001, SALES-002, PROC-002, PAYROLL-002, DIM-001 and RULE-001 are the ones that add such records).

## ACCT-003

An accountant can reclassify transactions in bulk between accounts or dimensions; in open periods this posts linked correcting entries. They can also write off small balances in bulk, flag adjusting entries, leave review notes, and see everything that changed since their last review with who changed it. Anyone with the posting grant reclassifies posted lines in bulk: each chosen line moves by its own posted correcting entry, dated on the original's date and linked to it, and the original stays as posted. A line in a locked month is refused (reopen the month first, CLOSE-005); a line moves once (move its correcting entry to move it again); bank, receivable, payable, sales tax and retained-earnings accounts are excluded on both sides; at most 200 lines move at a time. A bulk write-off settles every open invoice or bill whose balance is above zero and at most the chosen amount (or a chosen subset, at most 200 at a time) by its own posted credit note or vendor credit on a chosen ordinary income or expense account, dated in an open month on or after each item, in one batch that refuses if a payment lands meanwhile. Each item's balance is limited by the owner's per-item write-off limit ($100 until the owner sets one); a larger write-off uses INVOICE-008's separate marked credit preparation and ordinary exact-revision approval route; the bulk command still refuses it. Each batch is audited with its count and total and listed for the owner in Books › Review. Write-off credits are marked as bad debts, not returns: SALESTAX-004 keeps the gross sale, and SALESTAX-002 leaves them out except in a state where the accountant records a bad-debt deduction. On the cash basis, like any credit, the settled income share and the write-off appear together and net to zero. The adjusting flag is CLOSE-005's year-end flag. Review notes stay on their entries. "Changed since review" lists every audit event after the reviewer's own last "Mark reviewed now" (everything, before a first mark), newest first, with its author.

## MIGRATE-004

Recurring invoices, reminder settings, autopay customers, recurring bills and journals, bank rules (from QuickBooks Online's rules export and Xero's bank-rule export) and saved report definitions are carried over and listed. Imported bank rules retain RULE-002’s suggest/automatic mode, priority, split semantics and revision provenance, and every imported rule remains disabled until explicitly enabled. Autopay customers keep their saved methods when the same Stripe account is connected. Otherwise they are asked to authorize again, and no card or bank data moves between systems. Nothing sends, charges or posts until the owner turns it on. Vendor bank details held by QuickBooks Bill Pay, Xero's bill payments or another bill-pay service never move either. Pantry lists every vendor that service paid electronically in the last 12 months, with its open and upcoming bills. Once the owner connects Melio or BILL (BILLPAY-001), Pantry sends each listed vendor the provider's own request for payment details, with the owner's approval: Melio's "Ask vendor for payment details" email, or BILL's vendor invitation. Vendors enter their details at the provider, never in Pantry or in an email reply. Each request's status shows on the vendor (VENDOR-001), and the first payment to newly entered details follows BILLPAY-002's holds. A bill due before its vendor answers is listed 5 business days before its due date, and the owner chooses how to pay it: a check by mail through the provider, an ACH payment the provider schedules once the vendor answers, or a payment made outside and recorded (PAN-06). ## API-001

A versioned REST API with an OpenAPI document covers every record type. Owner-issued tokens are scoped by record type and grant, and writes follow the same approval and lock rules as people: an API-created journal is a draft unless the token holds the post grant. The API supports pagination, filters, idempotency keys and documented rate limits. The current scoped v1 door admits 120 requests per credential per fixed UTC minute, shared durably across Workers; discovery, replays and domain refusals count. Excess requests return 429 with remaining/reset headers and Retry-After, do no domain work and reserve no idempotency key. Missing, invalid and revoked credentials are refused before admission. Signed webhooks report created, approved, posted, paid, matched and locked events, with retries and a delivery log. Webhook policy (ct, 22 Oct 2026). Only the owner, in a native session, creates, edits, rotates, disables and deletes webhook subscriptions; agent subscription changes carry security and external and follow the company’s policy; other people need the owner’s subscription authority. A destination is an owner-entered https URL on the default port whose host is a DNS name under Base's literal/name public-host rule: IP literals, localhost, `.localhost`, `.local`, `.internal`, `.arpa` and single-label names, URL credentials and fragments are refused when entered and again before each send. Deliveries use Workers fetch with redirects refused (a 3xx is a failed attempt) and a ten-second bound; only the status code is read. Each subscription has a 256-bit secret, shown once at creation or rotation, sealed with the installation key and never logged, exported or archived; a rotation keeps the old and new secrets valid for 24 hours and sends one signature per valid secret. Each delivery carries `Pantry-Event-Id`, `Pantry-Timestamp` and `Pantry-Signature: v1=<hex HMAC-SHA256(secret, timestamp + "." + exact body bytes)>`, and API.md tells receivers to verify it and refuse timestamps older than five minutes. The body holds only the event id, type and time and the record kind, id, revision and status: no personal fields, memo, amounts or documents; receivers read details through a scoped token. The event is written in the same atomic batch as the write it reports and is delivered at least once, with the same event id on every attempt; order is not guaranteed. A failed attempt (timeout, network, TLS, redirect, non-2xx or refused destination) is retried 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours and 12 hours after the previous failure from a persisted schedule, then marked failed. Only the owner reads the delivery history: per attempt the event id and type, record reference, attempt number, time, outcome, status code, error class and duration, never request or response bodies or headers; Activity and history name the destination host only. Disabling or deleting a subscription ends its pending attempts as cancelled, and re-enabling never resends them. An attempt whose record was erased under ERASURE-001 is cancelled as `record_erased`. A restored installation brings subscriptions back disabled with no secret and no pending attempts; re-enabling issues a new secret. DATA-001 lists each subscription. Destination names are checked as written, not on the address they resolve to; API-001b carries that. Every current and future financial report route here inherits REPORT-002 basis selection, declared defaults and labels before shipping.

## API-002

Pantry answers a documented subset of the QuickBooks Online Accounting API (Account, Customer, Vendor, Item, Invoice, Payment, Bill, BillPayment, JournalEntry, Attachable, Class, Department and TaxCode, with query and change data capture) at `/qbo/v3/company/{realmId}` on the installation's address, authorized by an OAuth 2.0 token Pantry issues under API-001's scopes. It serves software whose QuickBooks address can be set by its user: - programs built on Intuit's own QuickBooks Online SDKs, which document a configurable base URL (`Config.BASE_URL_QBO` in the Java SDK, `IppConfiguration.BaseUrl.Qbo` in the .NET SDK); - open-source QuickBooks Online client libraries with a configurable base URL; - the business's own scripts, and HTTP steps in Crank, Zapier or Make. Shipping commercial apps whose QuickBooks sync is built on Intuit's fixed hosts (the API at `quickbooks.api.intuit.com` and Intuit's OAuth server at `appcenter.intuit.com`) cannot be pointed at Pantry by their customers. That includes Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan, and they connect through INTEG-003 and INTEG-005 to INTEG-009's named connectors instead. Pantry never describes API-002 as a route for them. Pantry publishes the list of tools tested against API-002, each with the exact setting it uses and the version tested. Writes follow API-001's rules. An unsupported entity or operation returns a QuickBooks-style error naming what is not supported.

## INTEG-003

In many US service businesses, jobs, invoices, payments and time start in a field-service or time-tracking app. Those apps sync only to QuickBooks Online or Xero, over Intuit's fixed hosts, which their customers cannot change (API-002). Pantry therefore connects to each app's own API or export files. Each named app has a production path that a business with no developer account completes from the connections page (CONNECT-001): - **Jobber: broker.** The business connects through Pantry Connect's public Jobber app, listed in Jobber's App Marketplace after Jobber's app review. A Jobber custom integration is capped at 5 accounts without Jobber's approval, so it is not the route. The owner signs in to Jobber and approves access. Pantry syncs clients, products and services, invoices, payments with refunds and tips, Jobber Payments payouts and timesheets. The file fallback is Jobber's CSV exports of invoices, payments, payouts and timesheets. - **Housecall Pro: own account on the MAX plan, file on other plans.** On MAX, Pantry uses an API key that the account admin creates, with Housecall Pro's webhooks. Pantry syncs customers, price-book items, jobs and invoices, payments with refunds and tips, and payouts. On other plans the route is files: the Payments Report export (its Summary and Payment Details files) and the job and invoice export. They are imported through a saved column mapping (MIGRATE-007), and Pantry shows when each file was last imported. Housecall Pro's Zapier triggers (New Completed Job, New Scheduled Job) carry no invoices or payments. Through INTEG-002 they only list completed jobs whose invoices the next file should bring, and a completed job still missing after 7 days is an exception. - **ServiceTitan: broker.** The business uses Pantry Connect's app, which ServiceTitan has approved for use by many tenants. ServiceTitan's API terms limit a custom application to one customer, so the business never registers one. The business's ServiceTitan admin connects the app under Settings › Integrations › API Application Access. Pantry syncs customers and locations, pricebook items, invoices, payments with refunds, and technician timesheets. - **QuickBooks Time: own account, or file.** The admin installs QuickBooks Time's API add-on and creates an access token, following steps Pantry shows. The file route is QuickBooks Time's timesheet export. Pantry syncs customers and job codes, mapped to Pantry customers and service items, and approved timesheets. - **Harvest: own account or broker.** The route is a personal access token with the account ID, or Pantry Connect's Harvest OAuth 2.0 app. Pantry syncs clients, projects, tasks, approved time entries and expenses. For a business that invoices in Harvest, it also syncs invoices and payments. - **Any other app** connects through its export file and a saved column mapping (MIGRATE-007), or through its own Zapier or Make triggers into INTEG-002's actions. Every connector follows the same rules: - **Mapped once, then posted by the connection.** On connecting, the owner maps the app's items, tax rates, payment methods and accounts to Pantry's items (ITEM-001), tax codes (SALESTAX-001) and clearing and deposit accounts (INTEG-004). The owner confirms customers matched by app ID, email or name. Approving that mapping revision is the approval for the invoices the app issues. Each synced invoice then posts as issued, labeled with the app and the mapping revision, with no second approval under PAN-04. An invoice waits in the connection's error queue and never posts when it has an unmapped item, a tax rate with no tax code, or a total that differs from its lines plus tax. A change to the mapping needs the owner's approval again. - **Idempotent on the app's IDs.** Every record keeps the app's ID and revision, so replaying a sync, a webhook or a file creates nothing twice. An app invoice keeps the app's number, behind a prefix per connection, so it never collides with Pantry's own sequence (INVOICE-001). - **Changes after sync are corrections.** An invoice edited in the app after it posted is corrected by a linked credit and reissue in an open period, and a void in the app voids it by credit. A deletion, or any change dated in a locked month, waits in the exception queue. Nothing is edited or deleted silently. - **Time waits on invoices.** Approved time entries arrive as INVOICE-007 time entries for their customer and service item. An entry the app has already invoiced arrives marked billed, so Pantry never bills it again. When the app does not invoice, the entry waits on the customer's next Pantry invoice. - **The app keeps chasing.** INVOICE-004 reminders and AGENT-008 leave an app-issued invoice alone, unless the owner turns them on for that connection. - **Starts after the cutover.** A connection's start date defaults to the day after the move-in cutover (PAN-16, MIGRATE-001 to MIGRATE-003), because the moved-in history already holds what the app synced to QuickBooks Online or Xero. Earlier records are skipped and counted. An invoice open at cutover is linked, by app ID or invoice number, to the moved-in open invoice, so a later payment in the app applies to it and nothing is created twice. - **Error queue and tie-out**, as in INTEG-001. Failures show with reasons and retry on the connections page (INTEG-002). A daily tie-out compares the app's invoiced, collected and refunded totals per day with the ledger, and any difference is a named exception.

## CONNECT-002

For providers that admit only approved partners, the Pantry project runs Pantry Connect. It holds the partner approvals and the production client IDs and secrets, and it passes each provider's security, compliance and annual reviews: Plaid's Compliance Center, Gusto's security and QA reviews, Melio's and BILL's partner programs, Intuit's app assessment, Xero's app partner certification, Rippling, MX and Finicity, Jobber's app review for its App Marketplace, ServiceTitan's approval of an app for many tenants, Google's OAuth app verification for the send-only Gmail scope and Microsoft's publisher verification. It also operates the mail domains of CONNECT-005 and CONNECT-006, with an email carrier account in good standing. - **A relay, not a store.** The provider's per-business tokens (Plaid access tokens, OAuth refresh tokens) are created into the installation and kept there, encrypted with the installation key as DATA-001 requires. Each call from the installation passes through the broker, which adds the partner secret, forwards the call and returns the response. The broker keeps only installation registrations, the provider connection IDs bound to each installation (so a token copied to another installation is refused) and the fee ledger. Its logs hold only installation ID, provider, endpoint, status and byte counts: never a token, transaction, document or payload. Mail passes through the same way: an outgoing message is held only until the carrier accepts it, and an incoming one only until the installation acknowledges it (at most 7 days while the installation is unreachable), then deleted. Only the message ID, a hash of each recipient for bounce and complaint handling, the outcome and the size are kept. - **Revocable.** An installation registers with the broker by a key pair generated at setup. The owner can revoke that registration, which makes every token the installation holds unusable. - **Fees at cost.** The broker charges only each provider's own per-connection fee at cost, for example Plaid's monthly fee per connected Item. It is billed to the business's card through Stripe and itemized monthly. There is no Pantry subscription. - **The books never depend on it.** Leaving the broker never touches the books: disconnecting keeps every line received, and statement files and file paths keep working. - **Open source and self-runnable.** Pantry Connect's code is in this repository. An accounting firm, or a business holding its own partner approvals, can run its own broker and point the installation at it. - **Visible health.** Broker health appears on the connections page (INTEG-002) and the dashboard. While the broker is down, its connections show "waiting for Pantry Connect", then resume without duplicates.

## CONNECT-005

Every email Pantry sends goes through one mail route the owner chooses on the connections page (INTEG-002). The sender name is the business's name, and Reply-To is the business's own address, which the owner confirms once by a link sent to it. ## ERASURE-001

The owner can erase a person's personal data. A customer, vendor or person with no posted transactions is removed. One with posted transactions keeps the legal name and amounts the books need. ## Operating rules

These rules bind every clause, including the integrations and agents.

- **One coherent book.** One legal entity per installation, with USD as the home currency, and double-entry books kept on the accrual basis. Cash-basis figures are calculated from those same lines (REPORT-002) and are never a second ledger. Money is integer cents; amounts arrive as decimal strings and are converted exactly; tax and line amounts round half up on integers.
- **Balanced, immutable postings.** Every posted entry has at least two lines and equal debits and credits. Posting, balance and period locks are enforced by the database's own triggers as well as the server. A posted entry never changes or disappears; it is corrected by a linked reversal or correcting entry.
- **Authority at the moment of writing.** Every handler resolves the signed-in person, agent token or connection and checks the capability it needs. The same check is a guard row inside the atomic D1 batch, so a role removed or a session revoked mid-request still stops the write. Route guards alone are never relied on.
- **Approvals name exact versions.** Posting a journal, approving an invoice, a bill or a payment, deciding an agent proposal, approving a rule or schedule, locking or reopening a period and signing off a move-in each record who approved which revision. A changed record makes the earlier review unusable.
- **Evidence, not inference.** Bank and card lines, provider events and documents are evidence. None of them posts by itself: posting comes from a person, an approved rule or schedule, or an agent permission the owner granted (AGENT-010), and every such posting names its source. Documents are kept byte for byte and treated as untrusted text. No figure is accepted from a person, provider or agent as a total: reports are always calculated from posted lines.
- **Providers hold the money.** Pantry never holds funds, card numbers or vendor bank accounts. It sends instructions to the buyer's own Stripe, Melio, BILL or payroll account and records the provider's state, which stays authoritative for settlement.
- **Locks bind everyone.** A locked month refuses drafts, postings, payments, matches, feed categorizations, rule and schedule postings, provider events and agent proposals dated in it, until a reopening request is approved by someone holding that grant. Late provider events for a locked month wait in an exception queue.
- **Nothing clears silently.** An unexplained difference is never written off to finish a reconciliation, a close or a sync. It stays a named exception with an owner until a person resolves it.
